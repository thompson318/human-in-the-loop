# Human in the loop simulation for novel technologies in healthcare (and beyond).

[Stephen Thompson](https://orcid.org/0000-0001-7286-1326) Senior Research Software Engineer,
[UCL Advanced Research Computing Centre](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/advanced-research-computing/)

I'm preparing a UKRI Research Software Engineering fellowship to create "human in the loop
simulations". The plan is to collaborate with researchers developing novel 
technologies that are intended to work collaboratively with humans. It is important that the
success of the technology will depend on how humans interact with it. The fellowship will 
fund me to create bespoke simulations of novel technologies at the very early stage of development.
The simulations will enable better understanding of how humans will interact with the technology and help
steer the translational research process. 

To strengthen the grant application I'm looking for some examples of translational research 
projects that will be running over the next 5 years. I can include projects that are already 
funded or that you're preparing grants for. 
I'm targeting interventional healthcare because 
that is my research background, but if you have an idea for how this could be applied to
your research outside this domain I'd like to hear about that to. 
There is also an [open component](#Open-Component-Games-for-outreach-and-teaching) that 
may be applicable if your project doesn't fit neatly elsewhere.

If that sounds interesting then please [contact me](mailto:s.thompson@ucl.ac.uk) to discuss 
further. At this stage all I need is a few lines that I could add as example projects. Or you
can read on for more information.

## More Information 
I expect my idea to be applicable to projects that:

 - are translational, aiming for first in human trials within the next 5 years.
 - are developing novel hardware or software intended to be used by a human (either directly 
by the patient or by a clinician) to perform an intervention.
 - the technology works in collaboration with human/s to produce an outcome.

To help with inspiration here are some examples I can think of from may past research, 
but this list is not meant to be exhaustive or restrictive; adding autonomy to surgical robotics,
 novel imaging devices (multi-spectral, photoacoustics, confocal microscopy), 
augmented reality displays, image mosaicking, things involving artificial intelligence. 

## What I can offer (if grant successful)

I'm targeting funding for research software engineering 
over five years to develop abstract simulations of novel technology that allow us to 
estimate the impact of system performance parameters on clinical outcomes. 
The idea is inspired by the [IDEAL recommendations](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(09)61116-8/abstract) and is intended to create an 
understanding of the likely impact of changes in system parameters on clinical 
outcomes at the earliest (stage 1) stages of development.

If funded we would:

 - work with you to identify key system parameters, modes of user interaction, and expected clinical outcomes.
 - provide around 3 months of research software engineering time to develop a relevant simulation.
 - fund a researcher at 10% FTE for 6 months to develop and deploy the simulation in a user study.
 - support with performing a user study and publishing the results, similar to my [previous work](#Related-Publications) in the area.
 - ongoing support over the life of your translation project for small modifications to the simulation to test development ideas.
 - a review (and publication) at the end of the project to see from  how the simulations impacted the translation project. 

## Open Component: Games for outreach and teaching
The simulations and games that I have developed and intend to create are very well suited
to use in outreach activities and teaching. I'm proposing running a parallel stream utilising
the same software components to deliver games for outreach and teaching. The aim of this
is to establish [UCL Advanced Research Computing (ARC)](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/advanced-research-computing/) as a centre of expertise in this area. So if you can think of a
game you would like to develop for outreach or teaching please [get in touch](mailto:s.thompson@ucl.ac.uk) to discuss your idea.
